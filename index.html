Puppy Vaccination Schedule: What New Owners Need to Know

By HappyPupGuide.

A puppy vaccination schedule is one of the first things a new owner should sort out with a vet. Vaccines protect a young puppy against serious diseases such as parvovirus, distemper and rabies, and they are given as a series of visits in the first months of life, rather than a single appointment. Your vet sets the exact timing for your puppy, based on its age, breed, health and where you live.

The first vet visit usually happens within a few days of bringing a puppy home. The vet will check the puppy over, talk through the vaccine series, and book the follow-up visits. Core vaccines are the ones most puppies need, and your vet may add others depending on your area and your puppy's lifestyle — for example, if it will meet many other dogs or spend time near water or wildlife.

Between visits, keep your puppy's exposure sensible. Until the vaccine series is complete, avoid busy dog parks, unknown dogs and areas where many dogs pass through, and carry your puppy in crowded places rather than letting it walk on the ground. Short, calm socialisation at home and with known, healthy, vaccinated dogs is a better plan than a crowded park at this age.

Costs vary by clinic and by area, so ask your vet for a clear estimate at the first visit, including the full series and any follow-up boosters. Keep the vaccination record somewhere safe and bring it to every appointment — it is the document boarding kennels, groomers, trainers and future vets will ask for. If your puppy misses a visit, call the clinic promptly rather than restarting the whole process on your own.
